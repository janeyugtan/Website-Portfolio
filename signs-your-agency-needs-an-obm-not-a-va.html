Signs your agency needs an OBM, not a VA

By Jane YugtanNovember 13, 20265 min read

Hi, I’m Jane. I’ve generated six figures in net revenue (USD) managing account health and client relationships for my clients, maintained 95% customer and client satisfaction in end-to-end project deliveries, reduced onboarding time by 87%, and reduced project delays by 30%.

I’ve served 10+ direct clients and supported 100+ client accounts in my 5 years working remotely as their Online Business Manager/Project Manager.

And I’ve seen how commonly founders and agency owners mistake hiring a VA for what they need, blurring the line when they actually need a true OBM. I’ve identified at least three core mistakes.

  1. Hiring a VA to buy back your time

    They wanted to expand and buy back their time to focus on the high-level priorities of the business, and a common solution is hiring a VA. There’s nothing wrong with that. In fact, this is smart. So here’s what happened.

    A VA got hired after a few weeks of the hiring process. While the VA was onboarding, founders thought they just needed a right hand to handle administrative tasks, the daily schedule, or communication with the team, contractors, and clients. However, during the onboarding week, the founders realized it was deeper than that. It’s like the Titanic hitting the tip of an iceberg. Problems resurfaced.

    Founders realized they needed someone who can work without hand-holding, take ownership, and make strategic decisions for them and the business. Someone who already knows what to do and what needs to get done in order to scale. And a general VA got overwhelmed, realizing the scope of responsibilities they didn’t sign up for, because they don’t have any idea how, and they are not built that way.

    So, that’s how you can differentiate what the business needs and make smart hiring decisions about what you’re looking for, because if not, it’s going to burn you in the end. The time spent onboarding them will cost you, and more often than not, it is not just the money.

  2. You become the bottleneck of your business

    This is such a true and common problem once founders or agency owners take on more projects than they can handle by themselves. Most founders know, or have the self-awareness, that they are the bottleneck because all of the decisions and approvals need to come from them. And that’s how project delays, client dissatisfaction, and the team’s communication gaps start.

    For example, a founder is traveling for a production shoot or a manufacturer visit abroad or in another state, but project deliverable timelines don’t stop just because he is traveling. So, while he wants to scale and network or work on other projects, other projects get delayed. The team doesn’t know who to contact in this situation, and the founder gets stretched too thin trying to manage everything on his own. It’s like bills: they don’t stop just because you are tired or don’t want to work today.

    If this ever happens, that’s when you know you need an OBM to handle communications for you while you are away. You need someone managing and handling the backend of the business while you focus on being in the front, growing and scaling.

  3. No system, no single source of truth

    Lastly, this is my favorite part, or the sweet spot I always unravel when they talk to me during a discovery call. The first question I always ask is if they already have a system in place, a project management tool, or a centralized system. And 70–75% of founders I’ve talked to said they had one before but are not using it, or they don’t have one at all.

    So, this is a dead giveaway that there is no one source of truth for what’s happening on every project, what the team is working on, or where we need to improve, stop, or reroute. Even processes or SOPs are a problem when they are too focused in the business and not on the business. It’s really a problem if you keep doing it, because you are not going to scale or grow to the place you want to be.

And those are just three core mistakes that let you instantly know for yourself that you need an OBM and not a VA. You need a second brain that will think and work for you strategically. And some founders don’t even know the difference between hiring a VA and an OBM.

You need to stop making these mistakes if you want to have more time for yourself to spend with family, to network with people, to drive client acquisition, and to make high-level decisions for the business.
